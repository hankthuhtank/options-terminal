An apparatus for reading the Bible as literature

VELLVM

Before it was doctrine it was an object — copied by hand onto animal skin, for a thousand years, by people who left notes in the margins. This is that book taken apart into the pieces you'd actually want: the text, the map, the people, the links between verses, and an easier way to understand it all.

✦

Nothing here is invented

Every verse, note, profile and cross-reference is fetched live from open APIs while you read. The coordinates on the map are real survey coordinates. If a thing on this page can't be traced to a source, it isn't on this page — and the one feature that generates text says so on its face.

Unrolling

Chronicle

Forty-eight stories in the order the book tells them, gathered into six quires. This is the spine of the whole thing — read only these and you'd know the plot. Click a folio to open that passage in the reading desk. Pass its collation in the Scriptorium and the folio turns gold.

Atlas

Almost everything happens in a strip of land you could drive across in a day. The places are drawn the way a medieval cartographer drew them — walled towns and scalloped hills — but sat on real survey coordinates from OpenBible.info. Drag with one finger to pan. Use the + / − buttons to zoom (or pinch on some devices). Double-tap to zoom in. Pick any name from the index to fly to it.

Register

Every named person in the Bible — 3,067 of them, not a curated few. Each carries their family links, how many verses name them, and Easton's 1897 dictionary entry where one exists. Click a name to open the entry; blue text inside it opens the passage; family names are clickable too, so you can walk a bloodline from Adam down.

Threads

The book quotes and answers itself constantly. This draws the 344,799-link cross-reference web out from whatever verse you pick — thicker line and closer to the centre means a stronger link, green is the Hebrew Bible, blue is the New Testament. Click any node to go read it.

Scriptorium

You're an apprentice. Before a copy left the workshop a second scribe read it against the exemplar line by line — that check was called a collation. Click any cell in the Great Quire to sit for its collation: eight questions built from the real text of that passage. Six right out of eight and the leaf is gilded for good.

Colophon

A colophon is the note a scribe left at the end saying who copied the book and from what. This is that. Everything here is fetched live from open data — nothing is hardcoded scripture, nothing is invented. These are the people whose work this is built on, and what they ask in return.
