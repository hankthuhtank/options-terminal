Module 01 / Combustion
Inside the engine

Four strokes.
Thousands of times a minute.

The engine looks complicated until you slow the cycle down. Watch the piston, valves, air and combustion interact, then step through what each stroke is actually doing.

Combustion chamber viewer● animation live

Gasoline engine

A four-stroke gasoline engine repeats intake, compression, power and exhaust. One full cycle takes two crankshaft revolutions.

01IntakePiston travels down. Intake valve opens. Fresh air/fuel charge enters.
02CompressBoth valves close. The piston squeezes the charge into a much smaller space.
03PowerCombustion rapidly expands gases and drives the piston downward.
04ExhaustExhaust valve opens. The piston pushes spent gases out of the cylinder.
What matters: The piston does not create rotation by itself. The connecting rod and crankshaft convert its up-and-down motion into rotary motion.
Real hardware

From animation to metal.

Once the cycle makes sense, real engines stop looking like a wall of parts. Cylinders, heads, cams, crankcase, intake and exhaust systems all exist to control that same basic sequence.

Module 02 / Power path
Where engine power goes

Follow the torque
to the tires.

Power does not jump from the engine to the road. It passes through a chain of components, each changing speed, torque, direction, or where that torque is sent.

Drivetrain path / RWD exampleready

Power sequence

Press play and follow the path in the same order a technician would trace it.

EngineCreates crankshaft torque.
Clutch / converterCouples engine to transmission.
TransmissionChanges gear ratio.
DriveshaftCarries torque rearward.
DifferentialTurns power 90° and splits it.
Axles + tiresApply torque to the road.
Transmission cutaway

Gear ratio is leverage.

Low gears trade speed for torque. High gears trade torque multiplication for wheel speed. The transmission lets one engine operate across crawling, accelerating and highway conditions.

Module 03 / Chassis
Control the vehicle

Keep the tire planted.
Stop it on command.

Suspension controls motion between the body and the road. Brakes turn vehicle motion into heat. Both systems make more sense when you can watch the hardware move.

Bay A / suspensionmovement view

Double wishbone

Upper and lower control arms guide the wheel through its travel. The spring supports the vehicle; the damper controls how quickly that movement happens.

Bay B / brakinghydraulic view

Disc brake

Hydraulic pressure pushes the caliper piston, clamping pads against the rotor. Friction converts kinetic energy into heat, slowing the wheel.

Module 04 / Electrical
Starting + charging

Mechanical energy
becomes electrical.

The battery gets the engine started. Once it is running, the alternator powers the electrical system and replenishes the battery. Learn the loop instead of memorizing parts.

Alternator cutaway

The alternator is belt-driven by the engine. A rotating magnetic field induces current in stationary windings, which is then rectified and regulated for the vehicle's DC electrical system.

StartBattery feeds the starter motor → starter cranks the engine.
RunEngine turns the alternator through the accessory belt.
ChargeAlternator supplies vehicle loads and restores battery charge.
GroundThe chassis/engine ground completes many electrical circuits.
Module 05 / Truck mode
Same physics, heavier job

Built around load,
traction and durability.

Trucks share the same fundamentals as cars, but the frame, axles, transfer case, suspension, cooling and diesel systems are designed around heavier loads and harsher duty cycles.

Rear axle

Cooling

Towing and diesel operation create heat, so cooling capacity becomes a major design priority.

Module 06 / Diagnostic bay
Learn the thought process

Don't guess.
Isolate the system.

A good diagnosis is a sequence of tests that narrows the possibilities. Run this basic no-start case and watch the evidence build instead of jumping straight to a part.

Will not start.

Diagnostic evidence
Choose your first test.
