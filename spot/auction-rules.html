TRUST / AUCTION RULES

Clear bids. Clear winners.

These rules explain exactly how placement auctions work, how winners are determined, and what happens if something goes wrong.

1. Each logo area is its own placement

An object may contain one or more separately auctioned logo areas. Each placement can have its own starting bid, minimum increment, dimensions, location, and bidding history. Unless explicitly stated otherwise, winning one placement does not give the bidder rights to any other area of the object.

The outlined area shown on the auction image is the intended placement zone. Minor physical differences may occur when a real sticker, decal, print, or other material is applied, but SPOT will use reasonable efforts to preserve the represented location and scale.

2. What counts as a valid bid

A valid bid must be submitted through the official SPOT interface by an eligible account, meet or exceed the minimum required amount, arrive before the placement closes, and comply with account, payment, and content rules.

Bid records maintained by SPOT's server control over browser display, screenshots, cached pages, or third-party messages.

3. How the winner is determined

The highest valid bid recorded at the close of the placement is the presumptive winner. If two bids have the same amount, the earlier valid server timestamp takes priority unless the auction specifically states a different tie rule.

The award remains subject to successful payment and approval of the winning brand asset under the Brand Content Policy.

4. Auction timing

Start and end times shown on SPOT are authoritative for that auction. Unless a live auction explicitly states that automatic extensions are enabled, bidders should assume the displayed closing time is final and should not rely on last-second bidding.

SPOT may extend or pause an auction when a significant outage, configuration error, payment-provider failure, suspected attack, or other event materially affects fair access to bidding.

5. Auction integrity

Shill bidding, fake accounts, coordinated price manipulation, payment fraud, bid suppression, automated abuse, account sharing used to evade restrictions, or bids placed without a genuine intent and ability to pay are prohibited.

SPOT may remove suspicious bids, request verification, suspend accounts, or cancel an affected placement where reasonably necessary to preserve auction integrity.

6. Errors and technical problems

If the displayed price, placement configuration, timer, or bid history is obviously incorrect because of a software or administrative error, SPOT may correct the error. If a problem could have materially changed bidding behavior, SPOT may pause, reopen, extend, or cancel the affected placement rather than enforce an unfair result.

7. Winning bidder obligations

A winning bidder must complete any required payment or balance by the deadline presented by SPOT. Failure to pay may result in loss of the placement and account restrictions. SPOT may then offer the placement to another bidder, reopen the placement, or otherwise dispose of it.

Brand artwork must be supplied in a usable format and must satisfy the Brand Content Policy. If a winning asset is rejected, the bidder may be given a reasonable opportunity to provide a compliant replacement before the award is cancelled.

Simple rule: bid only what you are prepared to pay, and only with a brand you have the right to use.
