The case

Every prompt has a compartment

A compositor's job case gives the letters you reach for most the biggest bins. These do the same: the wider the compartment, the more it holds. Pull one to fill the galley below, or search across everything. Prompts with dashed slots can be filled in place before you copy.

Bin width tracks how many prompts sit inside. Click to pull, click again to put back.

Composing stick

Set your own, one piece at a time

A compositor sets a line into a stick with a fixed measure, so every piece has to earn its width. Tick only the pieces your task needs. The assembly order is deliberate: supplied material first, then the task, then how to handle it.

Setting

The techniques that still pay

Ranked by how much they return rather than by how clever they are. The foundational four fix most problems and cost almost nothing. Everything below them is situational, and reaching for all of it at once is its own failure mode.

Then / now

Advice that quietly expired

Most prompt guides on the internet were written for models two generations back, and some of the best-known advice now costs you more than it gives. The clearest signal in the field: Anthropic removed over 80% of Claude Code's system prompt for the newest models with no measurable loss on their coding evaluations. Less scaffolding, more judgement.

The press

Connecting a model to real work

A prompt on its own is a proof pull. Everything here is the machinery that turns it into a run: how a model reaches your tools, how it learns your procedures, how multiple steps become one workflow, and how you know any of it is working.

Lockup

What holds, and what gives

A forme that is not locked tight throws type across the pressroom at speed. These are the failure modes worth understanding before you give an agent access to anything that matters. The first two are architectural, which means no amount of careful instruction fixes them.

    Why the pressroom words

    Movable type was the first technology for setting language into a fixed frame, where every piece took real space and the craft was in what you left out. The vocabulary already fit, so this guide borrowed it rather than inventing worse words.
